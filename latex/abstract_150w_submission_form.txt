Green banking policy frameworks coordinated through the Sustainable Banking and Finance Network (SBFN) assume that supervisory guidelines directing banks to price environmental risk will strengthen the link between firms' credit access and green investment. We test this using two independent World Bank Enterprise Survey samples: 28,042 firms across 41 economies on a seven-item green-practice outcome, and 89,797 firms across 162 economies on emissions monitoring. Across four estimators (pooled logit, Bayesian hierarchical, causal forest, country fixed effects), bank credit access is robustly associated with green adoption (13 percentage points), but neither SBFN adoption nor regulatory quality moderates that relationship; both instead shift the level of adoption directly. Effect heterogeneity is concentrated at firm size, not country institutions. A Callaway-Sant'Anna staggered event-study on an independent country panel (217 economies, 2000-2024) converges on the same conclusion. The binding constraint appears to be firm-level absorptive capacity rather than country-level regulatory capacity.
