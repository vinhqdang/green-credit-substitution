\title{Credible on Paper? Evaluating the Effect of Green Banking Policy on Firms' Green Investment in Emerging Economies}

\author[buv]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}

\author[bav]{Thi-Hong-Hanh Nguyen}
\ead{hanhnth1@hvnh.edu.vn}

\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}

\address[buv]{British University Vietnam, Hung Yen, Vietnam}
\address[bav]{Banking Academy Vietnam, Hanoi, Vietnam}

\begin{abstract}
More than sixty emerging and developing economies have adopted green banking guidelines through the Sustainable Banking and Finance Network (SBFN). The policy's theory of change holds that supervisory guidelines lead banks to price environmental risk, so that credit flows to firms investing in cleaner production. We evaluate two links in that chain. At firm level, using two independent World Bank Enterprise Survey samples (28{,}042 firms in 41 economies; 89{,}797 firms in 162 economies) and four estimators, bank credit is associated with 13-percentage-point higher green-practice adoption, but the association is the same in adopting and non-adopting economies (0.076 versus 0.072 in a causal forest) and does not vary with regulatory quality. At country level, a staggered-adoption event study of 217 economies finds no effect on renewable-energy share, and a CO2 association that appears to reflect which economies join. The guidelines show no detectable effect on the margin they target.
\end{abstract}
